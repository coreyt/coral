By the end of this exchange, the Customer has received "order confirmed" from the Checkout Service — the last of ten messages. That confirmation only happens because the Checkout Service first got "payment authorized" back from the Payment Gateway. The Payment Gateway, in turn, only sends that after the Card Network gives it an authorization code. The Card Network obtained that code after asking the Bank to verify funds and getting "funds available" in reply. Stepping back further, the Payment Gateway had already checked with the Fraud Service and gotten "risk score: low" before ever contacting the Card Network. And the whole chain was kicked off by the Customer submitting payment details to the Checkout Service, which in turn called the Payment Gateway with "authorize(amount, card token)".
